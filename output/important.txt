TITLE: Trading self-hypnosis (in progress) 
LAST MODIFIED: 2026-07-29 06:49 UTC

Not trading is sometimes the best trade. I prioritize a carefree state of mind over being anxious and fearful of missing out on trades. My ability to nurture and foster a carefree state of mind regarding trading is one of the most important factors in my success. When I maintain a carefree, free-flowing state of mind, I make better trading decisions and I have better ideas. I fully accept the risk involved with my trades. I am satisfied with the amount of trades I can initiate and maintain while having a carefree state of mind, knowing that there will always be other opportunities.

I have no reason to be anxious, put pressure on myself, or in other shape or form sabotage my careful state of mind. I have been there before. I experienced it all and now I know that it's not worth it.

I am satisfied with my returns. My returns are nicely compounding when I just trade according to my own capacity and drive. Sometimes it's better to be late in a trade than too early. Trading is reactive, not predictive.

I happily let go of past hurts or failures and mistakes in my trading history. Doing so I feel free and empowered. I believe in my success both as a trader and in my journey of life.

Woody Allen once said, "80% of success is showing up." I keep persevering, independent of my state of mind and mental bandwidth. I adapt successfully to my mental and emotional capabilities. I can capitalize in trading on what I already know.

I maintain an open, curious state of mind, free of the fear of missing out. I allow myself to completely withdraw from trading for a number of hours or days. I trust my risk management is in place. I am okay taking losses. I practice negotiating the loss in my mind before taking on the trade. Even when I operate mentally and/or emotionally on a subpar level - say I'm sleep-deprived, in pain, and anxious - I am committed to taking the loss that I negotiated beforehand. Doing it consistently will be easier over time. My focus is on high-probability trades with asymmetric risk/reward. I only take such trades. I recognize price action patterns, which gives me some edge.

For example when I look at a chart just based on the price action and RSI, I can make a more than 50% chance bet on price direction. I have other sorts of income I can rely on. I have the capacity for the necessary mental discipline and mental flexibility for trading and when I don't I do not trade. Doing so increases my return on investment, my yearly PnL, and it also keeps me calm and centered.

Having removed the fear of missing out, assuming full responsibility for all possible outcomes, and accepting the risk helps to create my own framework, my own system, and my own rules instead of relying on other people's predictions, advice and reasoning. Of course I incorporate those elements in my own framework.

I trust that by following these guidelines the evolution of my own framework will happen organically depending on my mental bandwidth. I am happy with the pace that my mind makes possible. I do have time.

I navigate the occasional struggles gracefully, knowing that they are part of the game and they are fueling my growth as a trader and as a human being.

I let go of the things that I cannot control or I do not comprehend in a certain situation. Having removed the pressure, my chances paradoxically increase that I understand more complex phenomena.

I discover, create, and adapt my style as a trader. Having these mental perspectives, the emotional maturity, the discipline, and the experience in place, I feel calm even when my mind is foggy, I cannot think clearly or I'm tired, in pain, or irritated. These perspectives transcend my transient and volatile mental states because I have worked long enough on solidifying them, reinforcing what's been working, and letting go of what wasn't.

I respect my mental bandwidth and my capability at any given time, knowing that if I miss out on trades there will always be other opportunities. I feel confident that having this perspective only increases my PNL.

Speaking from experience, I believe that in my particular case less is more when it comes to trading.

It is greatly soothing and relaxing that I can make trade-free time and space if I need to. I accept the transient limitations of my mind, for any reason. I let go of old patterns related to fight or flight states, urgency and pressure in trading. These were the necessary evils in my trading journey from a bygone era. Occasionally I remember them and I greet them with grace.

My vocabulary has also changed. Instead of "I must", "I need to", "I have to", I use "I want to", "I feel like", "I'm curious", "I'm inspired", with regard to making trades, monitoring positions, and staying in sync with the market.

These perspectives get deeply embedded in my psyche. They become my second nature. I do not chase. I do not conform. Instead I discover and express myself through trades in my chosen manner, which is intellectually satisfying and deeply rewarding, both on mental, emotional, and financial level.

I keep putting these perspectives into practice as long and as often as needed. I know that the reward is absolutely worth it. I'm doing my reps in a disciplined way, knowing that it is my way.

TITLE: MC'S INVESTING FRAMEWORK
LAST MODIFIED: 2026-07-27 11:51 UTC

FAQ & MC's INVESTING FRAMEWORKS

Some rules / frameworks to keep in mind for this chat, and for general investing & trading:

- Markets require a dedication and commitment to self-learning and improvement. Those who know themselves, build on their strengths, learn from weaknesses = win.

- Returns are inversely correlated with emotional state. Remove all emotion when making any decision in this arena.

- Process, discipline, and execution equals having rules + following them. Rules must be simple, clear and repeatable.

- In the long run, position sizing is more important than entry price. Size also tends to be correlated with emotional state, see prior bullet point.

- The #1 most important and immediate goal for any new investor/trader should be to NEVER risk a large loss. Understanding this goal, and how to achieve it early on, will increase the chance of success exponentially. Read on for more critical points on this.

- How much money you make is determined by: the market regime being favorable to your strategy (which you do not control), the number of high-quality opportunities available at any given time (which you do not control), and the risk-reward calculation of each individual trade (which you control).

- If you trade only the absolute best risk-reward setups with (for example) 5:1 or greater asymmetry, you can lose on 83% of your trades and still break even (profit factor of 1). Meaning, even if the market is terrible you may still be able to protect most of your capital. Now let's take it a step further. If the market is somewhat favorable and you keep taking only the most asymmetric trades, and you still manage to win on just 30-40% of them, your profit factor will explode to 2.14-3.33, which means you expect your profit on each trade to be double or triple your capital risked. This is excellent and in the top ranks of world-class traders. Now let's take it one more step. If you have "easy" market conditions (like we had in 2025 for the better part of 8 months), and 60-70% of asymmetric trades are winners -- then your profit factor is 10, meaning for every $1 at risk, you are making $10 in return. Experienced traders can earn millions in such conditions. This is why taking asymmetric trades is essential. Paul Tudor Jones (in the original Market Wizard book) said he only took trades with 5:1 or greater asymmetry... learn this early and do this forever. Following strict rules means that losing $1 on a single trade becomes irrelevant. It's the market's fee on the way to making $10 on a massive winner.

- Identifying and staying invested in monster winners will determine the majority of an investor's long-term returns.

- When trading, always make it your own. Size it relative to your risk profile.

- I don't use levered ETFs or options on levered ETFs. Liquid ETFs, Futures, and Stocks only. To each his own.

- When we put on a position, we always use stops.

- When initiating a position for a turn or reversal, stops are placed at the local high/low on a closing basis plus a small margin of error (you decide your risk tolerance and position sizing).

- When initiating a position for a trend, stops are placed at pivot or trend resistance/support on a closing basis plus a small margin of error (you decide your risk tolerance and position sizing).

- A view will lead to exploring a position, but only a clearly defined risk-reward setup will lead to allocating capital and putting on a position.

- After putting on a position as discussed, with the trade parameters clearly communicated, the view will not change based on intraday ticks, because the view did not create the trade. Only the risk-reward asymmetry created the trade. See prior bullet point.
